Kenya added 2.84 million fixed connections, but only eight counties are named

Kenya had 2.84 million fixed internet connections in the year to June 2026, up 32.4% on a year earlier. TechTrendsKE and Techweez both reported the figures in September, drawing on Communications Authority statistics. Neither gives a county split, so the headline cannot tell you where those connections are. A later report does name counties, and that is where my map can add something.

What the national numbers say

Fibre rose 29.7% to 1.57 million connections, which by my division is about 55% of the total. Satellite internet grew 54.4% to nearly 27,700, and Starlink accounts for 27,616 of those, about 1% of all fixed connections. Techweez adds that Safaricom leads fixed data with a 36.1% share. These count subscriptions, not people, and one connection can serve a whole household or office.

The eight counties with a waiting list

TechTrendsKE reported on 6 October 2026 that new residential Starlink orders remain restricted in eight counties, with buyers sent to a waiting list: Nairobi, Kiambu, Mombasa, Murang'a, Kirinyaga, Machakos, Kwale and Kilifi. The article blames capacity constraints. It gives no start date for the restriction and no capacity figure for any county. I found no second source for the county list, so I treat it as one outlet's reporting.

Where those counties sit on the map

All eight rank within the top 26 of 47 on my composite score, and none is in the lowest tier. Six of them have internet use above the 35% national figure. The two exceptions are Kwale and Kilifi, at 25.6% and 30.5%.

The two Coast counties stand out. Electricity access is 24.6% in Kwale and 27.8% in Kilifi, below the national 38.7%, yet buyers there are queuing for a satellite service. The article does not say who is ordering, so I will not guess. My post on the Kilifi and Kwale substations covers the power side.

Where towers are thin, the list is silent

8 counties have fewer than two cell towers per 100 km², and none of them is on the waiting list. That does not show nobody there uses satellite. It shows that the only county level fact reported so far sits at the better connected end of my ranking. The sparse counties are in my post on satellite to phone service.

What I do not know

No source I could read gives fixed internet or Starlink subscribers by county, so I cannot divide the 2.84 million by anything local. If the Authority publishes a county table I will add it to the map. Until then internet use from the 2022 survey stays my county measure, and it counts people, not subscriptions. TechTrendsKE also reports that Starlink's median download speed fell to 34.55 Mbps in March 2026 from roughly 47 Mbps a year earlier, citing Ookla data. I have not checked that figure against the Ookla source.
