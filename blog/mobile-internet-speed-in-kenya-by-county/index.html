Mobile internet speed in Kenya by county

By Paul Wamocha · 6 October 2026 · 2 min read

Nairobi has the fastest mobile internet among Kenyan counties with a reliable figure, at 62.1 Mbps average download. West Pokot is slowest at 16.0 Mbps. The median county sits at 32.8 Mbps, and 38 of 46 counties reach 25 Mbps.

The fastest counties

Nairobi (62.1 Mbps), Kitui (54.1 Mbps), Kwale (48.4 Mbps), Kiambu (47.7 Mbps) and Kajiado (44.9 Mbps) lead the table. Only 2 counties pass 50 Mbps. Kitui ranks second at 54.1 Mbps, ahead of Mombasa at 42.0, which surprised me. Only 268 tests sit behind Kitui's figure, against 2,465 for Mombasa, so a handful of fast connections can move it. It is a reminder that speed tests reflect who ran a test.

The slowest counties

West Pokot (16.0), Nyamira (17.5), Elgeyo Marakwet (18.5), Kisii (20.4) and Garissa (21.1) have the slowest average downloads. The fastest county is about 3.9 times as fast as the slowest.

Why I leave Samburu out

Samburu shows 80.2 Mbps, which would put it first by a wide margin. Its tower density is 1.3 per 100 km² and its internet use is 18.9%. Those numbers do not fit a county with the fastest mobile internet in the country. A few Starlink users in a small sample can lift an average, so I flag Samburu and drop it from the ranking.

How many tests sit behind each number

The sample size varies enormously. Kajiado has 13,164 tests and Kiambu has 11,736. Tana River has 63 and Baringo has 105. A figure built on a hundred tests is weaker than one built on ten thousand, and speed tests skew toward urban and higher income users. I expect rural speeds to be lower than shown.
